# Teacher Planning Platform — AI Use & Accuracy Notice

**Provider:** Brau Consulting LLC  
**Status:** Pre-Release Draft — Not Yet Effective  
**Post-pilot reconciliation:** 2026-08-14

Teacher Planning Platform ("TPP") includes generative artificial intelligence features intended to assist adult educators and authorized professional users with professional planning and, where specifically enabled, post-submission analysis of professional reflection content. This notice explains the role and limitations of those features.

## 1. AI assists; the educator decides

TPP may generate suggestions for learning targets, standards decomposition, Know/Understand/Do components, instructional activities, assessments, resources, literacy or ACT connections where appropriate, and other approved planning fields.

AI-generated planning content is a draft. The educator remains responsible for determining whether a suggestion is accurate, appropriate, lawful, aligned to applicable standards and local policy, pedagogically sound, and suitable for the educator's instructional context.

TPP's governed planning workflow is intended to preserve teacher control through review, edit, accept, and reject actions. AI-generated planning material should not silently overwrite saved teacher-approved planning content.

### Teacher-authored Weekly Reflection / PLC Discussion

The required Weekly Reflection / PLC Discussion is different from AI-assisted planning. Its approved prompts and required responses are teacher-authored. TPP does not use generative AI to suggest, generate, complete, or rewrite those required reflection responses.

After a teacher explicitly submits a completed weekly packet containing the teacher-authored reflection, a separate Reflection Intelligence feature may, where enabled and authorized:

- create a private teacher recap from that teacher's own submitted professional reflections; or
- create an anonymous, aggregate school PLC brief from submitted professional reflections when the minimum source threshold and authorization rules are satisfied.

Those post-submission summaries are AI-generated analytical aids. They do not replace the underlying teacher-authored reflection, do not become an official teacher response, and must not be used as teacher-quality, personnel, ranking, or performance scores.

## 2. AI can be wrong

Generative AI may produce inaccurate, incomplete, outdated, inconsistent, biased, misleading, or fabricated information. It may misunderstand curriculum or reflection context, overstate a pattern, or infer an alignment that is not supported by the authoritative standard or source material.

Do not rely on an AI response as the sole source for legal requirements, district policy, official standards text, safety requirements, special-education obligations, personnel matters, or other high-impact decisions.

## 3. Authoritative standards are distinct from AI interpretation

TPP is designed to ingest authoritative standards deterministically into governed snapshots with source/version provenance. Official standard text and provenance should remain distinguishable from AI-generated explanations, decompositions, alignments, or instructional suggestions.

An AI suggestion that a lesson aligns to a standard is an instructional planning aid. It does not amend the standard, replace the authoritative source, or establish official compliance.

Where TPP detects a change in an authoritative standards source, the change should be staged through the governed standards process rather than allowing AI to rewrite or silently replace approved source text.

## 4. No student data in AI requests

TPP's service boundary prohibits student personally identifiable information and student education records. Users must not place student data in prompts, lesson-planning fields, reflections, support material, or other professional content used by AI-enabled features.

AI requests should be limited to the minimum permitted professional context required for the approved feature, such as curriculum, standards, schedules, lesson plans, validation information, teacher-authored reflection content after submission, and generalized instructional needs that are not linked to identifiable students.

Reflection Intelligence includes additional product and prompt safeguards, but those safeguards do not authorize users to enter student data. The no-student-data rule remains the primary boundary.

## 5. AI service provider and model-training posture

TPP currently uses OpenAI's API/business services for generative AI features. OpenAI's current business terms and production account configuration must be reverified before this notice is published or materially relied upon in customer contracting.

Brau Consulting's production policy is not to opt TPP customer content into third-party model training without prior governance review, updated disclosures, and any required contractual or user approval.

Provider terms and production account configuration must be reverified before publication and whenever the AI provider or relevant data controls materially change.

## 6. Usage and operational records

TPP may log bounded AI operational metadata, including request status, model identifier, usage measurements, estimated cost, failure information, approved feature/event category, and educator decision state where applicable. These records support reliability, cost control, auditability, and product governance.

TPP must not intentionally place API keys, access tokens, service-role credentials, prohibited student data, or full reflection/planning content in ordinary AI operational telemetry when bounded metadata will suffice.

Separate product-adoption events for Reflection Intelligence may record content-free event keys such as private recap generated, school PLC brief generated, or PLC handout viewed. Those events are not reflection-content stores or teacher-performance measures.

## 7. Human review expectations

Before using AI-assisted planning content in an instructional plan or official school document, educators should:

- compare standards references with the authoritative source displayed or cited by TPP;
- verify facts, dates, calculations, citations, and resource references;
- review content for instructional appropriateness and local policy;
- revise language to reflect the educator's actual intent and classroom context; and
- reject unsupported or unnecessary suggestions.

For Reflection Intelligence, authorized users should treat generated recaps and aggregate PLC themes as discussion aids rather than verified findings about teacher effectiveness, student outcomes, or personnel performance.

## 8. Service changes

AI models and provider capabilities change over time. TPP may change models, prompts, guardrails, or providers when necessary for quality, security, cost, or service reliability, subject to the TPP Privacy Policy, institutional agreements, and governance requirements.

Material changes affecting data use, required reflection authorship, customer obligations, reporting audiences, or retained AI-generated content require corresponding privacy/legal/governance review before deployment.
